## Defender-side only (hard rule)
The supplier incident is told only as what the Compact sees: the supplier's notice (or lack of one), the Compact's own log lines, timestamps, decisions and outcomes. No intrusion mechanism, no technique research, no ATT&CK IDs beyond those already cited in the repo (and none is needed). Fixed content lists; nothing generated from attack research.

## Primitive: contract-clause negotiation (build it now)
- A fixed clause catalogue (game wording, written fresh; no text copied from any real contract or template): e.g. incident notification within a stated window, named security contact, log retention and access to logs relevant to the Compact, right to assess/audit (or accept an independent attestation), subcontractor disclosure, remote-access conditions (named accounts, MFA, session approval), data return and deletion at exit, vulnerability-fix timelines, insurance/indemnity — the last two flagged as counsel's and procurement's call, not the CISO's.
- Each clause has a negotiation cost (game: price uplift and/or "goodwill") and a risk effect; the supplier's tier decides which clauses are must-have. Budget comes from slice 1's leftover or a fixed procurement envelope (game numbers, labelled).
- Who decides: procurement and counsel own the contract; the business owner owns the service and its risk; the CISO sets the security requirements and advises. Any must-have clause dropped needs the business owner's signed risk acceptance (reuse the slice 1 acceptance primitive).

## District 2 content
- Tiering: 6–8 Compact suppliers (invented names, collision-searched; carry earlier lessons: the Library's remote-support vendor, the Clinic's scheduling platform, the Dispatch system supplier, the Airport's office IT services, a payroll provider, a print/mail house). Tier by criticality (what breaks for residents if it stops) and by data/access sensitivity — not by spend. Teach that the biggest invoice is not the most critical supplier.
- CSF 2.0 GV.SC subcategories (GV.SC-04, -05, -06, -07, -10): use the text ONLY if fetched from a NIST primary source (the CSF 2.0 reference tool / CPRT, or the Implementation Examples PDF already used in slice 1). Omit any whose text cannot be fetched; never paraphrase as a quote.
- Drills (3, `needs` on every verdict, several sources each):
  1. "Does this supplier have access to resident data?" — decisive: the data-flow record and the supplier's account list, not the supplier's questionnaire answer.
  2. "Is the supplier's attestation enough for our requirement?" — decisive: the attestation's scope and period against the requirement (a clean report that doesn't cover the service in use, or a period that has lapsed, is not enough). Do not name any real attestation scheme unless its scope wording is verified from its owner's own page; otherwise use a game-named attestation.
  3. "Did the supplier meet its notification clause?" — decisive: the clause's exact window and trigger wording, and the timestamps (supplier detection time as stated in their notice vs the time the notice reached the named contact). Teach that the clock starts where the contract says, not where either side prefers.
- Vendor incident (the set piece): a supplier tells the Compact of a security incident on its side. Contract A (with notification + log-access clauses) vs the real contract for that supplier (missing them). Show, in the Compact's own timeline, the hours lost asking for logs and confirming scope; the right actions are defender-side: identify what the supplier can reach (accounts, connections), suspend or restrict that access while evidence is preserved (blocking does not destroy evidence, so don't delay containment), ask for the specific facts the Compact needs, record every request and answer with times, decide nothing beyond the evidence (no "likely" as fact; no claims about the supplier's root cause), and return the access only when the evidence covers the latest activity. Counsel decides on notification duties; the CISO supplies facts on time. One decider per decision, whoever is on duty.
- Directive (game): procurement wants to sign a renewal today without the notification clause to keep the price; teach the business-owner acceptance route vs walking away vs a priced clause.
- Board: a Supplier round with the Compact Board (3 rounds) → an original badge (game, collision-searched).
- Dojo: Suppliers — 3 original puzzles (tiering, clause choice within budget, notification clock arithmetic).
- Scorecard (CAREER_LADDER §3 where it applies; otherwise labelled game metrics): critical suppliers with must-have clauses %, suppliers tiered with owner and review date %, notification-clause coverage of tier-1 suppliers %.
